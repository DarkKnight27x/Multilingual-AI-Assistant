## What it is
A Succession Certificate is issued by a civil court under the Indian
Succession Act, 1925. Unlike a Legal Heir Certificate, it is required
specifically to establish the right to inherit debts, securities, and
movable financial assets of a deceased person who died without a will.

## Documents typically required
- Death certificate of the deceased
- Proof of relationship to the deceased
- Details of the assets/debts/securities involved
- Identity and address proof of the applicant(s)

## General process
1. File a petition in the district civil court having jurisdiction over the
   deceased's place of residence (or where the assets are located).
2. Court issues a public notice inviting objections (usually a set
   waiting period).
3. If no objections, or after resolving them, the court grants the
   Succession Certificate.
4. This is a court process, not a simple administrative application — it
   is advisable to consult a lawyer.

## Fee
Court fee is generally a percentage of the value of the estate, subject to
state-specific court fee rules — verify with the local district court.

## Processing time
Can take several months due to the court notice/objection period — varies
by jurisdiction and case complexity.

## Where to apply
District Civil Court with jurisdiction over the deceased's residence.
