## What it is
A Consumer Complaint can be filed against a seller/service provider for
defective goods, deficient services, unfair trade practices, or
overcharging, under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

## Documents typically required
- Proof of purchase (bill/invoice/receipt)
- Any correspondence with the seller/service provider regarding the issue
- Identity and address proof of the complainant
- Details/evidence of the defect or deficiency (photos, warranty card,
  etc.)

## General process
1. First, try to resolve directly with the seller/service provider, and
   keep a record of that communication.
2. If unresolved, register a complaint on the National Consumer Helpline
   (NCH) portal or call 1915, for mediation-style resolution.
3. If still unresolved, file a formal complaint with the appropriate
   Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission — District Commission (for
   claims up to a threshold value), State Commission, or National
   Commission, depending on the claim amount.
4. Filing can be done online via the e-Daakhil portal.

## Fee
A nominal court fee applies for formal Commission complaints, scaled by
claim value — check the e-Daakhil portal for the current fee schedule.
The National Consumer Helpline (1915) itself is free.

## Where to apply
National Consumer Helpline (call 1915 or online), or e-Daakhil portal for
formal complaints to a Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission.
